## Definition

** Forests where more than 75% of canopy trees shed leaves during the dry season, typically occurring in areas with 600-2000mm rainfall and a pronounced dry period of 3-6 months.** 

These include moist deciduous forests dominated by Tectona grandis and Shorea robusta in areas with 1200-2000mm rainfall, dry deciduous forests with Anogeissus and Terminalia species in 600-1200mm zones, and thorn forests transitioning to savanna woodlands in areas below 600mm. They exhibit distinct seasonal rhythms with synchronous leaf fall during dry months (December-May), varying from complete deciduousness in dry zones to partial leaf retention in moister areas, and understory bamboo (Dendrocalamus, Bambusa) that may remain green. Regional patterns include sal forests in central and eastern India showing earlier leaf fall than teak forests of peninsular India.


## True and false colour composites characteristics

-  For the bands 543 (NIR-R-G) - bright red during the growing season, dropping to dull brown/grey when leafless.
- In comparison to evergreen, the red tones are more brighter and/or brown red tinged red. 
- For the bands 6-5-4 (SWIR-NIR-R) and 6-5-4Dry there is a difference in colour for decidious versus evergreen forest with dry season defined as the lower 25% percentile of the NDVI. 
- In comparison to evergreen forest, seasonal amplitude in vegetation indices (NDVI varying from 0.2-0.8) is diagnostic for deciduous forests, with peak greenness in September-October and minimum in March-April, varying by latitude and elevation.  


## Texture, shape and pattern

- Texture: Medium to coarse
-  Shape: Varrying 
- Pattern: Irregular
